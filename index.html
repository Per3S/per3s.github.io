Overview Register

Per3S is a french workshop centered on performances of storage system and all the issues related to storage at scale

The terminology used in Per3S for storage includes HPC systems as well as Cloud architectures, the common point between both being scalability. Presentations and talks focus in application, system or architecture. This 10th edition aims to gather during one day researchers from academia and industry, experimented or junior, storage users and customers with the sole purpose to exchange and foster the community.

Per3S 10th Anniversary Edition

Per3S is a workshop aiming to bring together the scientific and technological storage community to discuss and address issues and challenges associated to performance and data operations at scale. These topics cover HPC storage as well as Cloud-oriented architectures, both sharing the need for extreme scale.

Per3S fully encourages young researchers to present their work by submitting an abstract. The abstract can relate to an original work, on-going work, with fresh problems/solutions, or one already submitted and/or accepted in an international conference in order to be the subject of discussions.

9h-10h30 Session 1

9h00 Amphithéâtre Roussy

Rethinking the I/O Stack: Fast I/O Completion and User-Space Caching

Yu Liang

Fast storage exposes software overhead as a key bottleneck in both direct and buffered I/O. This talk presents UnICom (FAST 2026), which enables efficient direct I/O completion under mixed compute- and I/O-intensive workloads. It then draws on our HotStorage 2026 study to show why small buffered requests are particularly sensitive to software overhead, and to examine the challenges of bringing kernel page-cache benefits to user space.

9h30 Amphithéâtre Roussy

Carbon Footprint of Storage in Data Centers: the Impact of Using SSDs for Key-Value Stores

Thomas Ropars, INRIA

This presentation analyzes the lifecycle carbon footprint of SSDs versus HDDs for Key-Value stores to determine if SSDs' superior performance offsets their higher manufacturing and energy costs. By measuring hardware power consumption across various workloads, results show that SSDs typically achieve a lower overall carbon footprint due to their high operational energy efficiency. However, HDD-based systems remain the greener choice when SSD capabilities are underutilized or when the data center operates on low-carbon electricity.

10h00 Amphithéâtre Roussy

PaCaR: Improved Buffered I/O Locality on NUMA Systems with Page Cache Replication

Julien Sopena, LIP6

Multi-socket NUMA systems struggle with high memory access latencies, particularly in highly parallel, I/O-intensive applications where traditional migration techniques fail. To solve this, we introduce PaCaR, a transparent page cache replication mechanism that duplicates cached pages across nodes while ensuring write consistency and adapting to memory pressure without requiring application changes. Evaluations on a dual-socket server show PaCaR boosts performance by up to 1.4x in synthetic workloads and 25% in real-world applications. This paper received the Best Paper Award during EuroSys 2026

11h00 Amphithéatre Roussy

THEIA: Scalable and Effective Data Prefetching for Geo-Distributed Erasure-Coded Storage

Marc Tranzer, INRIA

Geo-distributed erasure-coded storage systems suffer from severe WAN latency bottlenecks during read operations, a challenge traditional prefetching methods fail to address. This presentation introduces THEIA, a scalable data prefetching framework that intelligently predicts and proactively retrieves data chunks across data centers. By effectively masking network latency and optimizing cross-datacenter bandwidth, THEIA significantly accelerates read performance for globally distributed applications while maintaining high storage efficiency.

11h20 Amphithéâtre Roussy

Impact of interference from concurrent jobs on checkpointing performance

Méline Trochin, INRIA / DDN

I/O has been identified as one of the main bottlenecks in HPC. Among the most I/O-intensive operations is checkpointing, which is necessary to save the state of an application and allow it to be restarted at an advanced stage of computation. However, near the parallel file system, concurrency prevents checkpoint phases from reaching the best I/O performance. In this paper, we study I/O interference in this specific context: we look at performance of a checkpoint phase when faced with different interference patterns, exploring aspects such as scale, number of processes, operation, number of files, etc. Through an extensive experimentation, in two systems, we show the impact of these aspects on checkpoint. Moreover, we show that some configurations --- e.g., an application that does random accesses --- lead to degraded system I/O performance.

11h40 Amphithéâtre Roussy

VoliStorM: A Crash-Consistent I/O Cache with Two Kernels Instead of Two Caches

Jana Toljaga, Telecom SudParis

A crash-consistent I/O cache is essential to ensure data integrity while optimizing performance. However, a general-purpose kernel fails to ensure integrity efficiently because of the high cost of synchronizing I/O requests with the many subsystems that rely on the cache. As a consequence, applications bypass the kernel I/O cache to implement a second cache in user space, which introduces overheads and high engineering cost. We address these drawbacks by doubling the number of kernels instead of doubling the number of caches. The first kernel is general-purpose, while the second provides an independent I/O stack tailored to efficiently ensure integrity. We implemented this design in VoliStorM using hardware virtualization. Our evaluation shows that, across databases, in-memory cache checkpointing, and language runtimes, VoliStorM outperforms the dual-cache approach while simplifying application code and improving code reuse.

Sven Breuner, VAST Data

Storage at Bull: The Alice Recoque Experience

Maxence Joulin, BULL

The Alice Recoque Exascale supercomputer bridges HPC and AI workloads through a unified, multi-tiered storage architecture. By combining a distributed all-flash NVMe performance tier with high-capacity object storage, the system eliminates I/O bottlenecks and guarantees deterministic latency at exabyte scale. Advanced data orchestration seamlessly manages complex data lifecycles, delivering the sustained bandwidth and data mobility required to drive true exascale discovery.

16h00-18h00 Session 3

16h00 Amphitéatre Roussy

TOTO: Transparent I/O Tuning for HPC Applications

Francieli Boito, INRIA

High-performance computing applications rely on parallel file systems, and their I/O performance is strongly affected by configuration parameters such as stripe count. However, the ideal stripe count is highly application- and system-dependent, making it difficult to predict and rarely tuned in practice. As a result, substantial I/O performance potential remains unexplored. We present TOTO, a transparent tool that improves I/O performance without requiring application modifications. TOTO intercepts POSIX calls, characterizes application behavior, and uses a machine learning model to select an appropriate stripe count, even for already opened files. We also introduce an allocation algorithm that balances performance and resource occupation, and describe a methodology for training the model once per system using limited data. Our results show that TOTO can improve I/O performance by up to 4.6 times compared to using a default stripe count, while imposing an overhead of at most 9%. Moreover, compared to the state of the art, TOTO can optimize more applications with a lower resource occupation, which is expected to decrease contention in the I/O infrastructure.

16h30 Amphithéatre Roussy

I/O-Cool: Using I/Os for Temperature Regulation When Training on the Edge

Jalil Boukhobza

This paper is motivated by the observation that edge data intensive applications, in particular AI training, alternate between I/O and compute phases. Notably, we found that processor temperatures consistently decrease during the I/O phases, revealing opportunities to optimize thermal behavior. We propose I/O-Cool, a proactive thermal management strategy for I/O–compute alternating edge workloads that leverages I/Os to maximize cooling opportunities while reducing the need for processor frequency reductions during the compute process

17h00 Amphitéatre Roussy

Closing Session: Reflection on storage from the last 10 years to the next ones!

Shadi Ibrahim

Over the past decade, the Performance and Scalability of Storage Systems (Per3S) workshop has served as a critical nexus for academia and industry to address extreme-scale data challenges. This 10-year retrospective examines the profound architectural shifts of the last ten years, including the rise of NVMe, flash-native distributed file systems, and the convergence of HPC and AI storage workloads. By reflecting on key breakthroughs presented throughout past editions, this session contextualizes how the community has successfully navigated the performance-capacity gap. Looking ahead, it outlines the emerging I/O, thermal, and data orchestration challenges that will define the next era of exascale and cloud-native storage infrastructures.
